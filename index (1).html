Why I built this

Every Christmas I fly home to China, and the thing my mum loves most is Whittaker’s chocolate. She doesn’t want me to spend too much, so I wait for it to go on sale. But it almost never does, and I don’t walk past the chocolate aisle often enough to catch the rare week it’s cheap. Most years I end up paying full price.

It isn’t only chocolate. Not long ago I bought a shampoo bar at full price, and two days later it was half off.

It’s not just me.

A day with Pantry Pilot

Meet Mia, 22. She studies in Sydney, shares a house in Newtown with two flatmates, and watches every dollar. It’s December 2030.

  1. 7:30 am

    Buy the right amount, not just the right item

    “Oat milk is 50% off. You’ll run out in 3 days. Buy 4, as many as you’ll finish before they expire.” Yoghurt is also on sale, but Pantry Pilot stays quiet: she has four tubs left.

    Deeper discounts mean bigger stock-ups, capped by shelf life and how fast she uses it.

  2. 12:40 pm

    Choose with real repurchase data

    40 shampoos on the shelf. Instead of star ratings she can’t trust, she sees: “71% of shoppers with similar habits bought this again.”

    Repurchase rate comes from real purchases, so it’s hard to fake. Out of stock? It suggests what loyal buyers switched to.

  3. 2:30 pm

    Never regret buying too early

    The conditioner she bought yesterday is 40% off today. $3.20 lands in her Everyday Rewards account. She didn’t have to do anything.

    Price-drop protection: if something drops in price within 7 days of buying it, the difference comes back automatically.

  4. 6:15 pm

    Share a pantry with flatmates

    Her flatmate added dish soap to their shared pantry. The cost is already split three ways.

    Shared household essentials, automatic cost splitting, no group-chat reminders.

  5. 9:00 pm

    Set a goal, and let it wait for the deal

    In October, Mia set a goal: 10 blocks of her mum’s favourite chocolate before she flies home on 20 December. It almost never goes on sale. Tonight it drops to half price, and Pantry Pilot buys all 10 and holds them for pickup.

    Goals with a deadline: say what you need and by when. It watches the price, buys automatically at the best moment, and in the last days tells you if now is as cheap as it gets.

  6. 31 Dec

    See what timing saved her

    “You saved $430 this year by buying at the right time.” She posts it. Three friends download the app.

    A year-in-review that Gen Z wants to share.

How it decides

Four models, each built on data Woolworths already holds.

  1. Usage

    Learns how fast each household uses each product from the gaps between purchases.

  2. Stock

    Estimates what’s left at home from what was bought and when. By 2030, 2D barcodes, which Woolworths already uses on fresh meat, record each item’s real expiry date at checkout, so stock goes from estimated to known. Users can correct it in one tap.

  3. Price

    Uses the internal promotions calendar for the next 4 weeks, and years of price history for seasonal patterns beyond that.

  4. Decision

    Combines the three into buy, how many, skip, or wait, with the reason shown every time.

From waiting for deals to creating them

Every goal tells Woolworths what shoppers want, how many, and by when. Pooled together, that becomes something no supermarket has had before: committed demand it can take to a brand.

  1. Shoppers set goals

    5,000 people are waiting for the same chocolate before Christmas, 8 blocks each on average.

  2. Woolworths sees the demand

    40,000 blocks of committed demand, visible weeks ahead.

  3. The brand funds a deal

    Woolworths offers the brand a short, targeted promotion with a known sales volume behind it.

  4. Goals fill automatically

    Every waiting shopper gets the deal, with no aisle-watching and no missed week.

Why it has to be Woolworths

Won’t customers spend less?

Shoppers already chase deals: switching to a cheaper supermarket is the most common way Australians cut grocery costs (ING, 2026). Pantry Pilot keeps the whole household’s routine shopping with Woolworths, so share of wallet goes up. Knowing who will buy what, and when, also makes demand forecasting sharper and cuts waste.

Doesn’t this clash with everyday low prices?

No. With or without promotions, Pantry Pilot times each household’s restock so they never run out or overbuy. When promotions do run, Woolworths knows in advance how many units each one will move, so promotions become predictable instead of a guess.

Can brands fake the ratings?

Most Australians now worry about fake reviews. Ranking is based on repurchase rate from verified purchases, not star ratings. Written reviews come only from real buyers and show how many times they bought it again.

Is it creepy?

It’s opt-in, every suggestion says why, and the pantry is editable. Customers control it, and the value they get back is visible in dollars saved.

What no third-party app can copy

  • The promotions calendar: Woolworths knows future prices, other apps can only guess.
  • Years of loyalty purchase data to learn each household’s real usage.
  • Verified repurchase data across thousands of products.
  • A delivery and click-and-collect network to turn a tap into an order.
  • Pooled demand from shoppers’ goals, which brands will fund promotions against.
  • A trust win that builds on Lower Shelf Price: honest, explained savings at a time when discount claims across the industry are under scrutiny.

Getting to 2030

Each step uses technology that exists today and builds on the one before.

  1. 2026

    Run-out reminders in the app, based on purchase history.

  2. 2027

    “Buy how many” suggestions on sale items, price-history charts, and 7-day price-drop protection for Everyday Rewards members.

  3. 2028

    Seasonal “wait” nudges and repurchase-based rankings. Expiry dates captured at checkout as 2D barcodes spread across categories, building on Woolworths’ own rollout.

  4. 2029

    Goals with deadlines, automatic buying and click-and-collect holds, plus shared household pantries.

  5. 2030

    Pooled-demand deals with brands, full household autopilot, and an open connection for customers’ personal AI assistants to order on their behalf.
